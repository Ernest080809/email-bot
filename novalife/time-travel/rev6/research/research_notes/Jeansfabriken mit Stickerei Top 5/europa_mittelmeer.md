## Which factories in these countries accept ~100 pieces per style for full-package denim with washing and in-house embroidery?

### Takeaway
I found **no factory where all four requirements are verified together**: ≤100 pcs/style, full package, in-house laundry and in-house embroidery. The closest fits:
- **Texko S.A.R.L. (Tunisia):** says it takes small orders, with a volume band starting at 100–500 pcs. Laundry and embroidery are unverified.
- **GMC Textiles (Tunisia):** core business is denim, and its development department covers embroidery and washes. MOQ is unknown.
- **LOGIC (Łódź, Poland):** has a dedicated denim line and states an MOQ of 50 pcs. Embroidery goes to partners and laundry isn't mentioned.
- **Aristo Jeans (Łódź, Poland):** has its own laundry, cutting room and sewing room and takes outside work. Embroidery and MOQ are unknown.

The large Tunisian and Moroccan denim exporters (Denim Authority, Sartex/Denim House, SIP, ETC, Blue Fingers) are vertically integrated with laundries, but their stated or listed minimums are 500+ pcs.

#### D. Duty and origin (Germany as importer)
- **Tunisia:** an EU implementing regulation adopted in late July 2025 applies **retroactively from 22 Jan 2025 for five years**. Within annual quotas, **cutting and sewing alone confer Tunisian origin**, so duty-free entry under the Association Agreement applies even with imported, non-originating fabric. The quota is **3,220,000 men's denim trousers** (and 1,515,000 women's) per year. Goods meeting the normal Protocol 4 rules stay duty-free without a quantity limit. — [Managers.tn](https://managers.tn/?p=100068); [La Presse (Tunisia)](https://www.lapresse.tn/?p=253160). (The Official Journal text was not retrieved.)
- **Morocco:** the "double transformation" rule applies. To enter duty-free, garments need EU, Turkish or Egyptian (cumulation) fabric. With Asian fabric "they pay a levy of 12%" (2020 source). [OLD] — [Just Style: How Morocco is redefining its role](https://www.just-style.com/features/how-morocco-is-redefining-its-role-in-fashion-production/)
- EU–Morocco Association Council **Decision No 1/2025 of 2 Oct 2025** amends the origin protocol. — [Lexaris: Decision 1/2025](https://www.lexaris.de/library/tableofcontents/10380687). The EU and partners apply transitional rules in parallel with the PEM Convention. — [EC TAXUD: PEM cumulation](https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/customs/international-affairs/pan-euro-mediterranean-cumulation-and-pem-convention_en)
- SGS (Feb 2026): Morocco, Tunisia, Egypt and Palestine **temporarily apply the 2021 transitional rules** with the EU and are expected to move fully to the revised PEM rules during 2026. — [SGS e-Customs](https://ecustoms.sgs.com/2026/02/12/trade-with-pem-countries-as-of-1-january-2026-under-revised-rules-of-origin/). The revised rules add origin-conferring processes for chapters 50–63. — [AEB](https://www.aeb.com/en/magazine/articles/free-trade-pem-rules-of-origin-agreement.php); details are in [TAXUD Guidance on revised PEM rules (Mar 2025)](https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/document/download/720d8a85-52a8-4d7c-8b0b-751d92153a1d_en?filename=Guidance-Revised-PEM-rules-of-origin.pdf).

- **No candidate is confirmed as a one-stop shop at 100 pcs.** In practice Novalife will face one of two set-ups:
  - A Tunisian full-package maker that accepts 100 pcs as a test order (Texko or GMC, possibly at a surcharge).
  - A Polish "assembled" chain: LOGIC or Aristo for sewing, a Łódź-area denim laundry, and a Łódź embroidery partner. This means more coordination but no customs.
- **Embroidery on cut panels before assembly** is easier when the embroidery shop sits in the same cluster. Łódź (PL) and Monastir/Ksar Hellal (TN) both have embroidery subcontractors (see above and Q3).
- **Duty:**
  - Poland, Italy, Bulgaria, Romania and Lithuania are in the EU: no customs.
  - North Macedonia (non-EU) was not researched for origin rules here.
  - Tunisia: 0% duty is possible with proof of origin, and the 2025 cut-and-sew derogation makes imported (e.g. Turkish or Asian) 12 oz denim workable within the quota.
  - Morocco: 0% only if the fabric qualifies (EU/TR/EG cumulation). Otherwise the 2020 source puts it at 12%.
  - From general knowledge, not sourced in this session: German import VAT (Einfuhrumsatzsteuer, 19%) and a customs declaration still apply to TN/MA imports, regardless of duty.

## Are Italian denim makers realistic at this volume and price (likely too expensive — quantify if sources allow)?

### Takeaway
Italy can technically do 100 pcs: there are small CMT workshops, a dense jeans district in the Marche with laundries and embroiderers, and a few claimed full-cycle makers. But the published price signals already sit at €25–45+ per piece for basic small-run denim, before Novalife's ~28,000-stitch embroidery, vintage wash with local abrasion, custom hardware and hand-set gold threads. **A €60 all-in target is unlikely in Italy.** This is an inference: no Italian quote for a comparable spec was found.

[SELF] — [Hoplites: jeans production](https://www.hoplites.eu/it/produzione-abbigliamento-conto-terzi/server-di-produzione-jeans-made-in-italy/); [Hoplites: laboratorio sartoriale](https://www.hoplites.eu/it/laboratorio-sartoriale/)
  - **X-MOD (Bologna):** full-cycle production for premium brands; "Se il design richiede stampe o ricami personalizzati, garantiamo anche questi servizi" (if the design needs custom prints or embroidery, we provide those too). Not denim-specific. [SELF] — [x-mod.it/servizi](https://www.x-mod.it/servizi/)

    - Lavanderia Adriatica: dyeing and washing of jeans; prototypes take 4–6 hours, denim garments up to 15–16 hours. [SELF] — [lavanderiaadriatica.com](https://lavanderiaadriatica.com/la-tintoria-di-lavanderia-adriatica/)
    - Blue Jeans Lavanderie Industriali: industrial washing and dyeing, all in Italy. [SELF] — [PV Marketplace](https://marketplace.premierevision.com/en/blue-jeans-lavanderie-3983.html)

### Inferences
- If basic small-run CMT plus wash already costs €25–45 and a quoted Italian "from" price is €40, then 12 oz rigid denim fabric, four-zone embroidery (~28k stitches), local abrasion, a custom shank button, rivets and the linen patch probably push the Italian landed cost **well above €60/pair**. This is an estimate. No embroidery-per-1,000-stitches or wash price for Italy was found.
- The Marche district structure (many micro-workshops plus separate laundries and embroiderers) suits an **agent-coordinated** small run rather than a single full-package factory.
- Italy is the strongest choice only if "Made in Italy" justifies a higher retail price. On cost, Poland and Tunisia are the better fits.

### Gaps
- No Italian quote, price list or embroidery price per 1,000 stitches was found. No Reddit or forum cost reports were found for Italian small runs.
- Hoplites (own factory or agent?), IFC's MOQ and Tim Service's current status remain unverified.

## Are Polish or other CEE jeans makers (there are several small-run jeans producers in Poland) a good fit, and do they embroider?

### Takeaway
**Poland is a real fit on MOQ and logistics, but not as a one-stop shop.**
- LOGIC (Łódź) states a 50-pc minimum with a dedicated denim line, but sends embroidery to partner firms and doesn't mention a laundry.
- Aristo Jeans (Łódź) has its own laundry, cutting and sewing for outside clients, but embroidery is unconfirmed.
- Dedicated Polish denim laundries exist (Pralnia Aktyw in Pabianice, PRALMEX-POL near Zielona Góra), so a Poland-only chain is feasible.
- Bulgaria and North Macedonia have denim history (Štip is the Macedonian textile hub) but only directory-level leads.
- Romania and Lithuania produced no named denim maker in this research.

### Inferences
- **The best Polish set-up:** LOGIC or Aristo for cutting and sewing, a Łódź-area embroiderer for the cut panels, and Pralnia Aktyw for the vintage wash and local abrasion. From general geography, Pabianice borders Łódź, so all three could sit within one metro area. This would be **EU-internal (no customs)**, reachable by train from Berlin for fittings, and LOGIC's stated MOQ of 50 is below Novalife's 100.
- **Downside:** Novalife (or LOGIC) must coordinate three parties. Embroidery has to happen between cutting and sewing, so LOGIC's existing "we deliver to and collect from decoration firms" service is the key point to confirm.
- Aristo's own laundry could remove one party. Ask whether it does local abrasion and has embroidery partners.
- Bulgaria, North Macedonia and Romania can't be recommended on current evidence. They may suit a second round once named factories with websites are found.

### Gaps
- No Polish MOQ for denim with embroidery specifically, and no prices, sample times or certifications (BSCI, GOTS, etc.) for any Polish candidate.
- Aristo's MOQ and embroidery capability are unknown. Whether Pralnia Aktyw and PRALMEX-POL accept 100-piece lots from a brand (rather than a factory) is unknown.
- Polish embroidery houses in Łódź were not researched by name (LOGIC's partners are unnamed).
- No named, verifiable denim factory was found in Romania or Lithuania.

### Inferences
- The lack of Reddit chatter is itself a signal. These EU and Mediterranean CMT houses mostly serve B2B brands, not the DIY-startup crowd that posts on r/streetwearstartup, so references have to come from the factories themselves, for example a request for photos of embroidered cut panels and the brands they have worked for.
- The third-party evidence that exists (Nudie, Guess, Scotch & Soda, MUD) points to **bulk-capable Tunisian factories**, not small-run specialists.

### Gaps
- No Reddit, forum or Instagram first-hand experience was found for any candidate. The search engine didn't surface Reddit, and direct Reddit or Instagram access was blocked (no fetching possible).
- Not checked: Instagram posts showing denim plus panel embroidery for LOGIC, Aristo, GMC, Texko or Denim House; Kingpins and Munich Fabric Start exhibitor lists; Polish forums (e.g. Wykop) and German founder forums.
